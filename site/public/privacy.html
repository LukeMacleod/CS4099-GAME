Prìobhaideachas

Privacy

Last reviewed 16 October 2026. We have tried to write this in plain terms rather than make it sound bigger than it is: today, Scotling Play collects almost nothing, because there is almost nothing to collect.

Draft, pending full legal review and the Child Safety / Compliance Officer's sign-off before anything here is treated as final. This page describes the current build honestly; it is not yet a complete privacy policy.

What we collect today, playing a game

Nothing that identifies a child. There are no pupil accounts, no login, no save files and no progress tracking in the current build. A class uses one shared class code; we do not know, and do not try to find out, which individual child is playing at any moment.

What we collect if you use the contact form

Only what the form asks for: a school or organisation name, a role, and an email address — from an adult, about themselves, not about any child. See that page: the form does not currently send anywhere live.

What we do not do

  • No advertising, and no third-party advertising or analytics trackers of any kind on this site or in the game.
  • No profiling of children, no geolocation, no behavioural nudges or streak mechanics designed to pull a child back in.
  • No selling or sharing of any data with third parties for marketing.
  • No use of pupil data to train any model.

Our Children's Code commitments

We are building against the ICO's Children's Code, treating all 15 of its standards as the checklist rather than a subset: best interests of the child as a primary consideration, high-privacy default settings, data minimisation, no profiling (off by default, not just "optional"), no geolocation, no nudge techniques, and transparent, genuinely usable parental/teacher controls once any account system exists. Whether the Code applies in the strict legal sense depends on exactly how a school deploys Scotling (schools acting as controllers for in-class use are often a different case from a direct-to-family product); we are applying its substance either way rather than relying on the narrowest reading of when it is required.

If a school is considering Scotling

We are preparing a Data Processing Agreement (UK GDPR Article 28) and a reusable Data Protection Impact Assessment input pack for councils' own legal and data-protection teams, because no single national template covers every Scottish authority. See For schools.

Contact about this policy

Use the contact page. As noted there, the form itself is not live yet — this will be updated once it is.
