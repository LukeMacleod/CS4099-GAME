For schools

Bring Gaelic into the games your pupils already play

Scotling Play is built for GME classrooms: shared Chromebooks and iPads, patchy island connectivity, 20-minute slots, and teachers with no spare time to become IT administrators. Here is the offer, how a school starts, who signs what, and what we can and cannot promise today — stated plainly, because a head teacher's time is worth more than a sales page.

Internal draft, 16 October 2026. Grounded in two DRAFT research briefs — R‑procurement‑and‑pricing and R‑compliance‑gates (15 Oct 2026) — and the dissertation's own evaluation chapter. Nothing below is a live service: see What exists today.

The offer

Scotling Play: short, Gaelic-medium mini-games for P1–P7 GME classes, playable in a single 20-minute slot, no login and nothing to install beyond a browser. No pupil accounts, no save data, no progress tracking today — so there is nothing about an individual child to protect beyond the session itself.

How a school starts — the realistic 15 minutes

"15 minutes" is what it takes a teacher to do their part. It is not a claim that procurement, legal review or a council's own sign-off processes take 15 minutes — those run on their own calendar, and we plan for that rather than pretend otherwise.

  1. Read this page and the evidence page (5 minutes) — what Scotling is, and the one classroom trial behind it, honestly stated, including its limits.
  2. Send the expression-of-interest form (5 minutes) — school, your role, and an email address. Nothing about any pupil. See Contact.
  3. We send a quote and the DPIA input pack — built for an invoice-and-purchase-order route, because, as the City of Edinburgh's own Devolved School Management scheme states, "In no instance providers' Terms and Conditions should be signed" by a school. There is no click-to-accept checkout to worry about, because it would not be something a head teacher is actually permitted to sign.
  4. First classroom session (5 minutes of setup) — a browser link. No Glow single sign-on required (confirmed not mandatory for a classroom resource), no SEEMiS integration, no pupil account creation.

Who holds the budget, and what it actually costs

In Scotland, digital classroom resources are a devolved budget line — the head teacher decides, without a council tender, for anything in the low thousands of pounds a year. A typical 200-pupil primary's whole discretionary classroom-materials line runs to roughly £5,000–£5,500 a year covering everything from pencils to CPD, so we are pricing to sit well inside it, not against it. Pupil Equity Funding (targeted at poverty-related attainment, not language enrichment on its own) is a second route some schools may prefer, spent in the same academic year it is awarded.

Exact price point: not yet set. The Founder Proxy and Partnerships/Funding Strategist size this against the budget figures above before it reaches the Founder Card.

What we are not asking a school to accept

  • No signed end-user licence or click-wrap terms from the school — we work by invoice and purchase order instead.
  • No Glow single sign-on dependency. No SEEMiS integration.
  • No claim of "Education Scotland approved", "SQA endorsed" or "Stòrlann approved" — none of those are real statuses for a classroom resource, and we will not imply otherwise.
  • No requirement to be on a Scottish procurement framework — none exists specifically for educational software, and none is legally required below the regulated-contract threshold.

Safeguarding and data, stated plainly

Safeguarding (PVG)

PVG membership under the Protection of Vulnerable Groups (Scotland) Act 2007 is required for anyone in a regulated role — chiefly, delivering training or instruction to pupils in person, or being a regular unaccompanied visitor to a school. Accessing data or systems about pupils is not, on its own, a regulated role under Disclosure Scotland's published guidance. No Scotling team member currently delivers anything to pupils in person, so no one currently holds PVG membership for this product — and if that changes (a classroom visit, a pilot session run in person), the named person gets PVG membership first. This is a Founder Card item under the studio's own rules before it happens, not something we would quietly skip.

Data protection

A council will expect a Data Processing Agreement under UK GDPR Article 28 and, where risk is judged high enough, a Data Protection Impact Assessment that the council leads and we populate. We are building a reusable DPIA input pack rather than waiting for each council to ask separately. There is no single national Scottish-schools DPA template, so expect to work from each council's own paperwork. See Privacy for what we collect today (currently: nothing about a pupil, because there are no pupil accounts).

Accessibility

We are building to WCAG 2.2 AA. It is not a legal requirement for a private supplier outside the Glow National App route, but it is the right bar, and it is expected in practice by councils' own accessibility and equality duties. See the accessibility statement for exactly where we are against it today.

What exists today

Honestly: a working build with about 12 minutes of games, a shared class code, real recorded narration, and no login, no save and no progress tracking — built and evaluated as part of a 2026 dissertation, not yet sold to any school. The expression-of-interest form on this site is wired to a clearly marked placeholder; nobody has bought anything yet, and nothing here is a live transaction.
